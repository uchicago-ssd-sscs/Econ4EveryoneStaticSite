The Crackle & Glow Case Study

Step into Maya's shoes. How should she spend her 5 hours?

Scenario

Economics is the study of how people make decisions about limited resources. A core idea in economics is marginal analysis, the practice of weighing the extra benefit of doing a little more of something against the extra cost of doing so. This kind of thinking helps people allocate their time, money, and energy as productively as possible.

Maya Chen works full time and runs a side business called Crackle & Glow, where she sells hand-poured soy candles through an online marketplace. She has decided to dedicate 5 additional hours per week to growing her business. Her challenge: how should she divide those hours between making candles and marketing?

Making candles means pouring, labeling, and packaging her products, which directly adds inventory to sell. Marketing means posting on social media, responding to customer reviews, and photographing her candles, which attracts new buyers and builds trust with existing customers. Both activities generate revenue, but time spent on one is time taken away from the other.

Now let's name what you just saw

Opportunity cost

The revenue Maya gives up by choosing one activity over the other. Every hour making candles is an hour not marketing, and vice versa. That is the "total cost" you watched rise and fall as you moved the slider.

Diminishing returns

As you added hours to one activity, its total benefit kept climbing, but by smaller and smaller amounts each time. That flattening curve is diminishing returns.

Marginal benefit (MB)

The additional revenue gained from spending one more hour on an activity. Calculate it by subtracting one hour's total benefit from the next hour's. MB shrinks as hours increase, which is diminishing returns in action.

Marginal cost (MC)

The additional revenue lost from spending one more hour on an activity. When MC exceeds MB for the next hour, that hour isn't worth taking, no matter how good total benefit still looks.
